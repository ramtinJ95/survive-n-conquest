# Wearing

A settler without clothing fetches a piece from a tailor's hut and wears it from then on. On its next look for work, once it has eaten and its home is stocked, it walks to the door of the nearest tailor's hut that holds a piece no other settler has spoken for, puts the piece on there, and the hut's store goes down by 1. It carries nothing on the way, and a settler with a job does it too. Its panel reads `Walking to the tailor's hut for clothing` on the way, and its `Cold nights` row ends in `, clothed` once it wears a piece, as in `None, clothed`. On the map a clothed settler wears a hood and a mantle of rawhide over the shoulders of its yellow tunic. Clothing is the third source of warmth: in a house without a fire a clothed settler is warm, and at the camp it is warm when the camp's fire burns, while a settler without clothing there has a cold night. This scenario plays a lodge, a tailor's hut, and a woodshed on seed 7, with no house, follows a settler that fetches a piece, and plays the first winter dusk at the camp's fire with clothed and unclothed settlers. `src/sim/wearing.test.ts` proves the rules, the order of tasks, and the six cases of warmth, `src/ui/selection.test.ts` the words, `src/render/swarm.test.ts` the hood, and `bun scripts/wearing-year.ts` prints the first year of the same rules.

## Sub-features

- `wearing-orders` gives every order from the toolbar at 08:00 on day 1: a Chop drag, a Quarry drag, a Stockpile drag, a woodshed, a tailor's hut, and a hunter's lodge, and no house.
- `wearing-hut` reads the tailor's hut's panel at paused stops 2 game minutes apart until it reads a piece, and one fewer once a settler has taken it.
- `wearing-walk` selects a settler that walks to the hut for a piece, reads `Walking to the tailor's hut for clothing`, `Carries Nothing`, and `Cold nights None`, and keeps its panel open until it reads `Cold nights None, clothed`.
- `wearing-dusk-walk` follows a settler whose walk the dusk cuts short to the camp, reads the piece still in the hut the next morning, and looks again for the settler that sets out for it, when that happens.
- `wearing-countdown` reads the hut's panel one piece lower after the walk.
- `wearing-layout` measures the settler's panel, with its clothing beside its cold nights, against the Stockpiles panel at 960 by 540.
- `wearing-pair` finds a settler who wears clothing and one who wears none in one paused frame at noon on day 10, at the near view.
- `wearing-fire` reads the camp's firewood 4 lower after the first winter dusk.
- `wearing-news` reads the day 10 line of the news, which names the settlers without clothing as having had a cold night, and none who wear it.
- `wearing-morning` reads on the morning of day 11 that each settler who wore clothing at the dusk has no cold night and each without has 1.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Open the game at `?seed=7&food=200`, so that nobody starves before winter.
- Zoom out a little. Press C and drag over the forest's edge east of the camp, press M and drag over the two rock tiles at the foot of the outcrop north of the camp, and press P and drag over a few tiles between the camp and the forest.
- Press K and click the ground just north of the camp for the woodshed. Press T and click west of it for the tailor's hut, and L and click below that for the hunter's lodge.
- Run at 8×. The huts stand on day 1 or 2. Click the tailor's hut and keep its panel open. Once the hunters have made two kills and the tailor has worked their hides, it reads 1 of 8 clothing.
- The tailor puts the first piece on at its own door. Watch for the next piece, then click the settler that walks from the camp to the hut, and keep its panel open until it reads that it wears a piece. Look at it on the map: it wears a brown hood and mantle.
- Run to the dusk of day 10 and watch the camp. The camp's fire takes 4 firewood, and the news names the settlers without the hood as having had a cold night. Click a settler the next morning to read its cold nights.

## Driving it with verify.mjs

Preconditions:

- `$V doctor` passes, or you use `$V run wearing`, which runs doctor itself.
- One page opens at 960 by 540 on `?seed=7&food=200` with the settlers, recorded, and `clock: 'paused'`, a few game minutes after 08:00.

- **Give the orders.** `takeForageView` takes the cold scenario's `COLD_ORDER_VIEW`, and gives the orders there, at 08:00 with the clock paused. It presses C, M, and P and drags each pair of `WEAR_ORDERS`, then presses K, T, and L and clicks the shed, the tailor's hut, and the lodge. `at day 1 08:00 the orders mark 35 trees, 2 rock tiles, and 9 stockpile tiles, and place a woodshed site, a tailor's hut site, and a hunter's lodge site, and no house` reads the status line after each order, the site labels, and the clock, which has not moved. It takes `wearing-orders.png` and wheels `COLD_CLOSER` in to the cold scenario's near view of 27 m.
- **Watch the hut.** At 12:00 on day 2 it clicks `WEAR_SPOTS.hut`. `at ... the tailor's hut stands, names its tailor, and holds no clothing` reads the panel. With the panel open it steps the clock `WEAR_STEP`, 2 game minutes, at a time, and stops at the first stop whose `Clothing` row differs, by 12:00 on day 7 at most. When it rises, it takes `wearing-hut-piece.png` and clicks the fullest cells of tunic pixels, up to 4 times 2 game minutes apart, until a panel reads `Walking to the tailor's hut for clothing`. When none does, it clicks the hut again and goes on watching. `with the tailor's hut's panel open, the hut reads a piece as the tailor makes it, and one fewer when a settler takes it` lists every change, and takes the walk's own countdown when the watch missed the tailor's first piece.
- **Follow the walk.** `at ... <name>, selected by a click, walks to the tailor's hut for a piece, carries nothing, and wears no clothing` reads the panel it found. `at 960x540 the settler's panel, with its clothing beside its cold nights, keeps clear of the Stockpiles panel and shows every row` compares their boxes and the panel's scroll height, and it takes `wearing-walking.png`. It steps 2 game minutes at a time and reads the panel at each stop. When the dusk cuts the walk short, the panel reads `Walking to the camp`, and the scenario takes `wearing-dusk-walk.png`, clicks the hut in the night, steps 30 game minutes at a time with the hut's panel open until its tailor is out again, and searches again for a settler on its way to the hut. `the dusk falls on <name>'s walk to the hut: it gives the walk up for the camp, and the piece waits in the hut through the night` needs the walker's panel to read no clothing and a piece in the hut's panel in the night, when nobody takes one, and names the first settler found on its way the next morning, who may be another. `<name> walks to the hut's door, puts the piece on there, and its panel reads that it wears clothing and carries nothing` needs `, clothed` after a stop on the walk, and it takes `wearing-worn.png`. `the panel keeps its width through the walk` compares the widths by day. `at ... the tailor's hut's panel reads one piece fewer than when <name> set out for it` clicks the hut again.
- **Side by side.** At 12:00 on day 10 it takes `wearing-pair.png` with nothing selected, then clicks the fullest 24 cells of tunic pixels. `at ... a click finds a settler who wears clothing and one who wears none in the same paused frame, at the near view` lists each settler, the pixel that selected it, and its `Cold nights`.
- **The winter dusk.** At 17:00 it clicks the camp and takes `wearing-before-dusk.png`, then steps the clock `COLD_LISTEN_STEP`, 2 game minutes, at a time to 18:30, reading the news at every stop. It takes `wearing-news.png` and clicks the camp again. `at ... the camp's fire has burnt 4 firewood on the first winter night` compares the camp's `Firewood` row.
- **The morning after.** At 07:00, 07:30, 08:00, and 09:00 on day 11 it clicks the fullest 24 cells of tunic pixels until it has read 8 settlers. `the news of the first winter dusk at the camp names the settlers without clothing as having had a cold night, and none of those who wear clothing` reads every text the day 10 line took while the scenario listened, since a line shows for only 30 s of wall time, and needs every name in them to be a settler read without clothing at noon or in the morning, none read with clothing at noon, and every settler read without clothing in the morning among them. `on the morning after, each settler that wore clothing at the dusk reads no cold night, and each without reads 1 cold night` reads `Cold nights` without its clothing words, and needs at least one settler of each group among those it found.
- **The clips.** `wearing-fetch.gif` runs from the click that found the walk that ended in the piece put on, with that settler's panel open, to the stop after the piece is put on, cropped by `WEAR_CLIPS.fetch` to the clock, the Stockpiles panel, the hut, the camp, and the selection panel, with 32 colors at 560 pixels wide. `wearing-dusk.gif` runs from the stop at 17:00 to the stop at 18:30 on day 10, with 48 colors at 640 pixels wide. Both are made with `decimate`: every frame that repeats the one before is dropped and the rest play at 8 a second, so a paused stop passes in one frame and a run plays at the pace SwiftShader drew it, several times the game's own. Say so wherever the clips are shown. The clock in each frame says its game time.
- **Proof.** Run `$V run wearing`. The evidence folder holds the clips, the screenshots, and `result.json` with every check, including `no page errors`.

## Gotchas

- The orders and the near view are the cold scenario's, so every pixel in `WEAR_ORDERS` and the camp's spots comes from the `scripts/aim.ts` commands above `COLD_SEARCH`. The tailor's hut and the lodge take the pixels of that scenario's two houses, and the hut's own click pixels come from the command above `WEAR_SEARCH`.
- The dusk ends every task, and a walk to the hut gives back the piece it spoke for. The next morning the piece goes to the first settler to look for work, which need not be the one the dusk stopped. On rj-firebat Ulvar set out at 19:10 on day 3, two minutes before the dusk, and at 03:48 on day 4 Elsi, the woodcutter, was the one on her way for it.
- The tailor stands at the hut's door when it makes a piece, so it puts the first piece on at once, with no walk. The scenario's search finds no walker for that piece, reads the hut 1 lower, and watches for the next.
- The news keeps a line for 30 s of wall time, whether the clock runs or not. From the last settler's arrival at the camp at the dusk of day 10 to the winter dawn at 06:36 is about 23 s at 8× on rj-firebat, so a reading of the news on the morning after can find the line gone. The scenario reads the line at 18:30 on day 10, and reads the morning after in the settlers' panels.
- Clothing never comes off, so a settler whose `Cold nights` ends in `, clothed` at noon wore it at the dusk, and one without those words in the morning wore none at the dusk. A settler without them at noon can put a piece on before the dusk, so the checks take the noon's `None` only for the names the news gives.
- On rj-firebat a run took 19 minutes, and the fetch clip took 1.8 MB and the dusk clip 3.7 MB.
- The settler's panel is 256 pixels wide since pull request 4.2's fix for the panels, and a row of its own for clothing made it overlap the Stockpiles panel at its longest readings, 256x288 from y 196 against the panel's end at y 210. The clothing reads in the `Cold nights` row instead, whose longest reading, `2, cold, clothed`, `SETTLER_LONGEST` in the `selecting` scenario measures.
- The settlers who wear clothing show about half as many tunic pixels as the others, because the mantle covers the top of the tunic. A search by tunic still finds them, a little later in the order of the fullest cells.
